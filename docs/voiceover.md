# Voice-over scripts

Two films, both 58.6 seconds. Read at a steady, unhurried pace. There is
silence built into each stage on purpose: the viewer needs a moment to look at
the people before you tell them the number.

Every stage runs 5.2 seconds in the same three parts, so once you have the
rhythm you can feel it rather than watch a clock.

| Part | Length | On screen | You |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Read | 2.4s | The line appears. Nobody moves. | Say the line |
| Leave | 1.4s | Those people fade away. | Say nothing. Let it land. |
| Settle | 1.4s | The number changes, once, and holds. | Say the number |

Times below are where each line **starts**.

## Reading notes

**Slow down on the numbers.** The number is the payoff of each stage. Say it on
its own, with a beat of silence either side, so it sounds like a tally being
marked rather than the tail of the sentence before it.

**Don't perform the losses.** Flat and matter-of-fact lands harder than
sympathetic, because the pictures are already doing the emotional work.

**The pause before "none of them told you why" is the whole film.** Leave a
full second. That line is the reason the diagnostic exists.

**Film two should sound lighter but not triumphant.** Same pace, slightly
warmer. The point is that it was the same twenty-four people, not that you
performed a miracle.

## Captions

Both films are silent and the lines are already on screen, so they work muted.
If you burn in captions for the voice-over, keep them at the very bottom and
clear of the number, or they will fight with it.

## If you want a shorter cut

For thirty seconds, keep the hook, Replying, Booking and Coming back, then the
end and the call to action. Those are the three stages that cost the most and
the one that hurts most.
